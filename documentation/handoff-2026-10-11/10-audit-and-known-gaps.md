# Independent audit and continuation priorities

> Historical: the first audit (14 October 2026). The current audit is [chapter 112](112-final-stage-exit-audit.md).

## First audit pass

1. Verify the exact executable, deployed DLL, probe and selected banks against the current pins. Confirm the live processes and physical clone rather than trusting a stale status record.
2. Read the complete 105-item movement checklist. Compare authoring candidates, actual game recordings, semantic binding and visual acceptance separately.
3. Review original footage for the latest carry stance correction and the rejected v26/v28/v29 comparisons. Inspect grip, posture, timing and physics; do not rely on the aggregate pass flag.
4. Trace the damage firewall/friendly-fire filter and existing authoritative damage adapters together. Confirm that protection neither duplicates damage nor blocks a legitimate intended enemy outcome path.
5. Check source-thread and lifetime ownership in callbacks, deferred initialization, pause, loading, peer loss and entity generation changes. Inspect non-atomic probe assumptions.
6. Review native source readers against Ghidra, exact assets and independent observations. A function name or source comment is not sufficient evidence by itself.
7. Confirm that release/UI/network claims remain limited: no full online two-account session, no complete native host/join flow and no general campaign authority are claimed.

## Broader co-op gaps

Enemies and much of the world still simulate independently outside bounded authority experiments. General enemy AI/health/death/spawn, projectiles, corpses/physics, inventory, doors, pickups, objectives, dialogue and mission decisions need consistent ownership and replication. General saves/checkpoints, cutscene entry/timeline, maps and campaign boundaries remain incomplete. Native session UI, Steam invitations and separate-account online connections remain unfinished.

Friendly fire is proven for attributed native health-entry cases and tested sword contacts. Every power/projectile, indirect physics/environment hazards, incapacitation and pre-damage takedown behavior still need coverage. An earlier unexplained unattended death is retained and cannot be assigned a cause without damage observations.

## Documentation limitations to audit explicitly

- Old READMEs/state sections sometimes say “current” about historical builds. They are copied as dated evidence, not silently modernized.
- The initial Ghidra journal has only early inventory additions; many later findings live in bookmarks. Both are exported.
- Native structures are partial minimum known views. Unknown bytes and semantic hypotheses remain unknown.
- Function metadata/decompiler prototypes can be incomplete. One-byte placeholder bodies and secondary unwind ranges have previously misled analysis.
- Source-address inventory includes data constants and potential false positives; it is not an approved API registry.
- Some old source/video links may be missing or renamed. The generation audit records missing local links; do not manufacture missing evidence.
- The documentation folder does not package the game, saves, tool binaries or every large telemetry file. Those remain referenced in the workspace.
- No complete historical skill invocation log or unsaved chat/tool history exists in the inspected artifacts. Tool/skill availability must not be confused with actual historical use.

## User constraints that remain binding

Use native game UI, native Steam networking, native local first-person behavior and the preferred native Doppelganger presentation. Keep original Steam assets/saves protected and preserve working milestones. Use windowed, low-setting, side-by-side automated testing on one PC for now. No ordinary milestone approval pauses. The current phase ends with a complete usable movement port and reviewable actual-game library, not just a candidate count. The user will review the animation library and provide visual feedback.
